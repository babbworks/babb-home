A. Beat & posture

Editorial lead for Manufacturing and Building. Co-shapes the editorial slate with Editor B. and runs the field crew on rotation for both Worlds. Wrote the Hamilton steel mill standing story from the call that opened it through the four-day shoot it’s currently anchored on.

Practical posture: spend the days. Most of Editor A’s pieces start with a phone call to someone they already know, get scoped on a second call, and don’t go to press until the crew has been on site for three days at minimum. Comfortable with audio-first reporting; collaborates closely with D.L. (sound) and B.K. (photography) on every standing story.

B. How to work with them

Open to pitches from inside Manufacturing or Building — long-form, mini-doc treatments, or single Daily-format dispatches. Less open to columns or recurring beats from outside contributors (those go through Editor B.). Will take a phone call before an email any day; will not take a deck without a phone call first.

Editorial workflow: drafts get one accuracy pass and one register pass; field notes stay public; glossary terms get hover-popups so the prose doesn’t apologize for being specific. Photographs are selected with B.K. Subjects have right of refusal on every photograph and quote, per BABB-FIELD 0.2.

C. Before Babb

Six years on the steel + rail beat at a regional trade publication, two of those years as a contributing editor. Wrote the Hamilton harbour series in 2022 that essentially became the prototype for what Babb Places now look like. Came over to Babb in 2024-08 to do the same kind of work at a publication that ships its own standards and tools.

Earlier: copy desk at a daily, two years as a wire-service stringer, a lifelong habit of standing in places where work is happening and asking who’s in charge. Born in Hamilton; the harbour is the room they grew up in.
