Ch 01 Arrival on the floor
00:00
QHow long have you been on this floor?
00:12
AThirty years next March. Started on the second — finishing-line scrap — moved to the first for about a year, then the third when my nephew came on as an apprentice.
00:38
QWhy move to the third?
00:42
AThe third’s honest. Nobody’s pretending. You can teach somebody at three in the morning who you can’t teach at three in the afternoon.
01:08
A"You learn the floor when you stop expecting it to behave. The floor doesn’t behave. The floor is fine. You have to be fine with the floor."
02:22
QAnd the people you came up with — are they still here?
02:30
ATwo of them. One retired in ’19. The other’s on first now, runs the caster end. The rest moved on or moved out.
Ch 02 The apprenticeship
06:40
QWhat do you teach somebody the first six months?
06:48
AHow to stand. Where to stand. When not to stand. Then the panels — charge, blow, tap, cast — in the same order, every time, until the order is in your hands.
08:14
AThe numbers come later. The numbers are easy. The standing is hard.
09:08
AI tell them: your first six months you’re going to feel useless. Good. Stay useless until you’re not.
10:22
QWhat can’t you teach?
10:30
AYou can’t teach somebody when to be quiet. You can show them. They get it or they don’t.
12:48
ADee, the new one — came over from the auto plant in February. Quiet. Watches everything. I’m not worried about Dee.
Ch 03 Automation
14:20
QWhat does the new console do that the old one didn’t?
14:42
A"The console keeps the number. We keep everything else."
16:18
AThe Workpads we’re using now — the foreman’s pad, the second’s pad — those make the handoff faster. Used to take eleven minutes. Now it’s seven, eight. The same conversation, fewer interruptions.
19:30
QAnything you don’t like about the new tools?
19:36
AI don’t like talking to the pad more than the people. We’re still working that out.
22:48
AThe 2028 rebuild — the vessel rebuild — that’s going to be the test. Whether all this stuff helps or whether it gets in the way.
Ch 04 The town
26:55
QWhere do you live?
27:00
AEast end. Walking distance. Half my street worked here at some point.
28:10
A"You don’t leave the mill at the end of a shift. You bring it home and it cooks down all night."
31:42
AThe town isn’t the mill, but the mill is part of how the town counts itself. When we hire, the east end knows by Sunday. When we lay off, the east end knew on Friday.
35:18
QIf the mill closed tomorrow, what would the town look like in five years?
35:30
ADifferent. Not worse, necessarily. Just different. But it’s not closing tomorrow. It’s not closing this decade. Beyond that — ask me in 2028.
38:00
QAnything I should have asked you that I didn’t?
38:06
AGet the foreman on second shift on tape. Hers is the version I learned from.
